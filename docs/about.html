About Springfield, Montana

"Where the Rocky Mountain Front Meets the Great Plains"

Geographic Location

Springfield is located in north-central Montana, nestled in the foothills where the Rocky Mountain Front meets the Great Plains. The municipality sits along the Sun River, approximately 22 miles southwest of Great Falls, the state's third-largest city.

Springfield's geography is characterized by rolling hills transitioning to mountainous terrain to the west. The Sun River provides a natural water source and created the fertile valley where the city developed.

History

Founding Era (1862-1899)

1862: First documented settlement by fur trappers establishing a trading post. The location became known as "Spring Field" due to numerous natural springs.

1867: William Thaddeus Cartwright established the first permanent homestead and sawmill.

1885: Great Northern Railway arrived, transforming Springfield from frontier settlement to commercial hub.

1886: Town incorporated as the Village of Springfield with Cartwright elected as first mayor.

1892: Great fire destroyed 14 buildings; community rebuilt with brick and stone, establishing architectural character that persists today.

Growth Period (1900-1949)

1902: City upgraded from village to municipal status.

1911: Carnegie Library opens on Elm Street.

1924: Springfield Dam completed, creating Lake Springfield.

1938: Cascade Manufacturing Company founded, becoming major employer.

1947: Springfield Regional Hospital opens.

Modern Development (1950-Present)

1961: Springfield High School opens on modern campus.

1995: Springfield Cultural Center opens.

2018: Historic downtown district placed on National Register of Historic Places.

2021: Springfield Public Library wins National Medal for Museum and Library Service.

2022: Rails to Trails conversion completed.

Climate

Springfield has a semi-arid continental climate with four distinct seasons. Chinook winds provide periodic winter warming.

  • Average Low: 35°F (2°C)
